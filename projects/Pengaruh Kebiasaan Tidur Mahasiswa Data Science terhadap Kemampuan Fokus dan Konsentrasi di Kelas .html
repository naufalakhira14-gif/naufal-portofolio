STATISTICAL ANALYSIS · STUDENT RESEARCH

Sleep Pattern & Learning Focus Analysis

Statistical analysis of sleep habits and learning concentration among Data Science students at BINUS University, Kemanggisan Campus. Survey data is used to explore sleep patterns and examine the relationship between late-night sleep habits, sleep quality, and learning focus through statistical analysis and regression.

Python Pandas NumPy Statsmodels OLS Regression WLS Regression

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Project Overview

This project analyzes sleep patterns and learning concentration among Data Science students at BINUS University, Kemanggisan Campus, using survey data. The analysis explores students' sleep habits, perceived sleep quality, and learning focus. Statistical methods and regression models are used to examine relationships between the variables without assuming a statistically significant effect.

02 / OBJECTIVE

Project Objective

Sleep & Focus Analysis

Analyze sleep patterns and learning concentration among Data Science students using survey responses.

Instrument Evaluation

Perform validity and reliability testing to evaluate the research instrument before statistical modeling.

Statistical Modeling

Analyze relationships between sleep-related variables and learning focus using OLS and WLS regression.

03 / DATASET

05 / STATISTICAL METHODS

Statistical Techniques

Descriptive Statistics

Summarize survey responses to understand the distribution and average values of sleep-related and learning-focus variables.

Validity & Reliability

Use Corrected Item-Total Correlation (CITC) and Cronbach's Alpha to assess the research instrument.

OLS Regression

Ordinary Least Squares regression is used to estimate relationships between the selected independent variables and learning focus.

WLS Regression

Weighted Least Squares regression is used to estimate the model while accounting for the sample weights based on student cohort strata.

07 / KEY FINDINGS

Key Findings

01

Students showed a tendency to sleep after midnight, with an average score of 4.05 on a 1–5 Likert scale.

02

Satisfaction with sleep quality was relatively low, with an average score of 2.79.

03

The average ability to focus while a lecturer explains course material was 3.36, while the ability to concentrate for a long period was 3.21.

04

The OLS model produced an R-squared of 0.027, while the WLS model produced an R-squared of 0.039.

05

Both regression models were not statistically significant at the 5% significance level. Therefore, the analysis did not find sufficient evidence of a statistically significant relationship between the selected sleep-pattern variables and learning focus in this sample.
